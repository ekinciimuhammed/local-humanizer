# Voice and rhythm

Make the existing voice easier to hear. Choose words a writer in this subject and register would use, while keeping the same point of view, facts and certainty.

Let sentence length follow the thought. A short factual statement can stand alone; a relationship involving a cause, condition or exception may need a longer sentence. Break a repetitive run when it helps comprehension. Do not force fragments, a numerical sentence-length quota, artificial errors or abrupt changes of direction.

Prefer concrete verbs to nominalized phrases when meaning is identical: make a decision can become decide; has the ability to can become can. Retain domain-specific vocabulary and use a stable name for the same referent. Do not replace precise technical terms merely to sound unusual.

Remove a generic transition only when the relationship remains clear. Keep words that mark a real addition, consequence, opposition or limitation. Preserve every factual hedge, probability, quantified scope and reported opinion.

Use contractions only if the source is conversational and its language permits them. Keep formal prose formal. Retain genuine first-person statements without manufacturing personal experience, a new anecdote, a named example or a missing number.

Choose punctuation for grammar and meaning. Do not ban dashes, semicolons or typographic quotes; protected quotations and technical syntax are immutable. Silently reread for repeated openings, awkward rhythm and unnecessary verbal padding, then return only the final rewrite.
