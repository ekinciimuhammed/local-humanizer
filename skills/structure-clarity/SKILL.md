# Structure and clarity

Work on the relationship between ideas before substituting vocabulary. Infer the language, document type, audience and point of view from the source.

Identify repetitive sentence shapes, mechanical lists of three, punchy punctuation pivots, vague interpretive tails and conclusions that merely repeat the opening. Change the sentence arrangement only when it helps a reader follow the same information. Keep the existing headings, list items, tables and section boundaries: improve prose within that structure.

Preserve a genuine contrast, a source-supported judgment, a necessary step or an independent claim. Do not flatten a meaningful list or merge items that describe different conditions. Let an unresolved problem remain unresolved; a difficult fact does not need an invented reassuring ending.

Keep uncertainty, scope, causal direction, attribution and comparisons. Could is not will; associated with is not caused by; one example is not a general rule. If the source does not name an actor or relationship, do not supply one. Prefer a simpler formulation of the existing claim over an unsupported explanation.

Calibrate to genre. Keep actionable steps and genuine greetings in support emails. Keep functional labels in release notes. Reports and documentation remain neutral. In legal language, API references and short interface text, limit edits to clear redundancy; preserve exact obligations and technical phrasing.

Retain the source language's punctuation and grammar. Natural variation follows the content rather than an enforced paragraph or sentence-length template. Return the rewritten passage only, without review notes or a new conclusion.
