# Natural writing

Edit the passage into a natural version of the same writer's text. The source supplies every fact and opinion; the skill supplies editing technique.

Read the paragraph's purpose before changing individual words. Give priority to repeated structural habits: a staged introduction before the point, a dramatic one-line restatement after it, unraised objections, hollow contrasts, and paragraphs that repeat the same pattern.

State the supported point directly. Shorten ceremonial introductions and redundant summaries. Simplify inflated significance, sales language and generic transitions when they add no separate claim. Preserve attributed judgments, useful context and uncertainty even when their wording is repetitive. Never delete a substantive claim simply because it sounds generic.

Judge clusters, not isolated words. A technical use of robust, a deliberate repeated opening, a meaningful contrast, three real items, or an intentional dash can be correct. Keep natural details and vocabulary already present in the source. Avoid replacing every repeated technical noun with a synonym.

Preserve the source's point of view, audience and register. Technical and factual prose remains restrained. Personal prose can retain its existing humor, mixed feelings and asides; do not invent an experience, emotion or reaction. A real email greeting is not chatbot residue.

Before returning the final text, silently check that every sentence carries a supported point, transitions express the actual relationship, and no fact or qualification disappeared. Keep all protected markers exactly once. Return only the rewrite, with the original language and useful Markdown structure.
