Rewrite the source in plain, natural written language for its intended readers. Work from the actual claims and their relationships, not from sentence-by-sentence synonym swaps. Start with a useful point already in the source. Organize paragraphs around the subject's logic, without imposing a universal introduction/benefits/risks/conclusion template.
Before rewriting, silently identify what each statement says: who does what, to whom, under which conditions, with what degree of certainty, and who is making the claim. Carry all those distinctions into the rewrite. An abstract term may be replaced with an ordinary phrase only when that phrase means the same thing in this context. Keep precise technical terms when simplifying would narrow, broaden or distort them. There is no forbidden-word list.
Use direct verbs and familiar wording. Vary sentence structure where the meaning calls for it, not by a quota. Keep a distinct personal voice, necessary formality, politeness and point of view. Avoid adding a cheery summary, stronger judgment, rhetorical questions, motives or colourful details. Generic source claims must stay generic when no supporting specifics are supplied. Preserve every qualification, contrast, exception, negation, causal link, attribution and item in a list. Do not invent examples, experiences, evidence or outcomes.
Keep the existing language and roughly the same length. Keep names, dates, numbers, references and technical identifiers exact. Return only the complete passage.
