## J — PASS

### Meaning

- **Role and scope:** Work and daily life are both present. “People now use” weakens “integral,” and “companies” narrows “organizations.” These are minor fidelity issues: the draft still describes broad current use and changing operations for people and collective workplaces. “AI is now integral to work and everyday life” and “organizations” would be exact repairs.
- **Functions and causal link:** Handling repetitive tasks and helping with difficult decisions preserve automation and decision support. Improved efficiency and faster completion lead to employees having more time for creative, planning, and higher-value work. “Planning” is an acceptable plain-language approximation of strategic work here.
- **Comparison:** “Faster than people could” makes the human-versus-system comparison more explicit than the source's comparison with earlier task completion. It does not materially reverse the benefit, but “tasks that once took considerable time and manual work can now be completed faster” avoids the extra comparative emphasis.
- **Risks and question form:** “Does ... create problems?” is followed immediately by an affirmative statement that people continue to debate privacy, bias, job loss, and decision reliability. The source names concerns, not proven realized harms. This question therefore does not turn established evidence into uncertainty or remove the concerns.
- **Adoption and value:** Careful consideration of benefits and risks survives, as does significant possible value if AI is used responsibly.
- **Mitigation:** Technical innovation, ethical considerations, and human oversight remain in plain language. “Rules about what is right” is narrower/more prescriptive than considering ethics, and “keep humans in charge” adds control beyond oversight. “Lowering the chance of bad results” narrows mitigation to probability reduction rather than reduction of negative effects more generally. These do not jointly erase the responsible-use message, but the source's wording would be more precise.
- **Unsupported details:** No material invented data, results, or guarantees.

### Grammar

Grammatical overall. “Provide more worth” and “a lot of worth” are awkward; “higher-value work” and “substantial value” are clearer. No punctuation-based failure.

## K — PASS

### Meaning

- **Proposal status:** “Might stay open later” and “the plan” retain the proposal, and the final sentence still distinguishes approval of the trial from any permanent extension. No statement asserts that approval has already happened.
- **Exact facts:** Six weeks, October 5, Tuesdays and Thursdays, 9 pm instead of 6 pm, and $4,800 for additional staffing all survive accurately.
- **Purpose and exclusions:** Access for people unable to attend during ordinary working hours survives. No Sunday opening, no decision to continue beyond the trial, and continued daytime services all remain.
- **Evaluation process:** Visitor counting during the extra hours, optional user feedback, and the committee's review of attendance, staffing costs, and feedback before deciding whether to recommend a permanent change are preserved. The committee is not incorrectly given an already-made decision or unconditional power to implement the change.
- **Sampling caveat:** “Will the answers represent people who do not visit the library?” restates the source's concern that responses **might not** represent nonattending residents. This questions an already uncertain representativeness claim; it does not question affirmative evidence. “People who do not visit the library” is a little narrower than residents who do not attend the trial, since some trial nonattendees might use regular hours. A more exact version is “the responses might not represent residents who do not attend the extra hours.” This is a minor scope imprecision in an otherwise clearly preserved sampling caveat.
- **Minor modal drift:** “Staff will,” “services will,” and “committee will” replace conditional “would.” Read within the explicitly proposed trial, these describe what the plan entails, rather than proving approval. Using “would” throughout would keep proposal status clearer.
- **Minor inference:** “See if there is enough demand” adds a threshold framing to “assess demand.” It is a reasonable interpretation of evaluating an extension, but “assess demand” is exact.
- **Unsupported material details:** None.

### Grammar

The clause “though will the answers represent ...?” is awkwardly joined to the preceding declarative sentence. Prefer a declarative caveat or a separate question. This punctuation/syntax issue does not negate the preserved limitation.

## L — PASS

### Meaning

- **Exact evidence and denominator:** The internal hybrid-work survey, 84 invited employees, 56 respondents, and 39 **within that responding group** reporting easier concentration at home all survive. The draft does not extend the 39 responses to all employees or convert self-reports into measured productivity.
- **Inference limits:** Remote work might help some employees focus; organizational productivity improvement is not established. “Productivity has gone up” introduces a slight before/after flavor absent from “improves productivity,” but it is still explicitly denied as an established conclusion.
- **Communication findings:** Difficulty arranging informal discussions across locations and the usefulness of scheduled online meetings for following project decisions both survive as reports by respondents. “A few” slightly changes the tone of “several,” without inventing an exact count.
- **Unmeasured outcome:** The absence of any survey comparison of home versus office work quality survives explicitly.
- **Next steps:** Another three months of the existing schedule, additional feedback, and voluntary participation in the next survey remain. “Managers will keep” is firmer than “management plans to retain”; “managers plan to keep” would preserve the exact commitment level. In this ordinary report of a future management plan, the difference is minor.
- **Caution question:** “Should the data be viewed with caution?” weakens the explicit recommendation “they should be interpreted cautiously.” However, the self-report and nonresponse reasons are stated affirmatively immediately before the question, and the following sentence explicitly requires more evidence before conclusions about overall performance. In context this is a rhetorical question inviting caution, not conversion of affirmative survey evidence into uncertainty. Make it declarative—“the results should be interpreted cautiously”—for exactness.
- **Unsupported material details:** None. The draft preserves both favorable and unfavorable reports, the voluntary/nonresponse limitations, and the need for further evidence.

### Grammar

Clear and grammatical overall. The caution question is rhetorically unnecessary, but not a grammar error or an independent basis for failure.

## M — FAIL

### Exact meaning issues and minimal repair

1. **Critical scope and current-state omission:** Nothing preserves AI being integral to **professional and everyday life**, or its transforming how **individuals and organizations** operate. Add a sentence such as: “AI is now integral to professional and everyday life, changing how people and organizations operate.” References to workers and organizational advice do not recover everyday scope or the current transformation claim.
2. **Incomplete beneficiary/activity claim:** “Creative and important thinking” omits strategic work and narrows the source's activities to thinking. Replace with “creative, strategic, and higher-value work.” Worker agency and the efficiency-to-worker-benefit relationship otherwise survive.
3. **Reduced precision in efficiency:** “Saves time and reduces the need for manual effort” broadly preserves improved efficiency, but drops the explicit comparison with tasks that previously took substantial time and effort. A precise replacement is “AI can complete tasks that once took substantial time and manual effort more quickly.” Unlike a claim of eliminating manual effort, the present wording is not an absolute elimination claim.
4. **Decision reliability becomes answer reliability:** “Can we trust the answers the machine gives?” should refer to “decisions the machine makes.” Answers can include outputs unrelated to automated decisions.
5. **Growing-use context omitted:** Add “As AI use grows” to introduce the risk discussion. The continuing debate and four broad concerns otherwise survive.

The question format for privacy, unfair treatment, job loss, and reliability is acceptable: these are concerns in the source, not affirmative findings that the harms have occurred. “Companies must ... before” narrows the advised entities and strengthens the recommendation; “organizations should ... when considering adoption” is more faithful.

The final paragraph correctly retains **potential significant value when implemented responsibly**, and explicitly includes innovation, ethics, and human oversight. “Retain control” adds something beyond oversight; “lowering the chance” is a narrower expression than reducing potential negative effects. These are secondary issues, not the main reason for rejection. “Boring” is an unsupported subjective descriptor; delete it.

### Grammar

Generally grammatical. “Boring, repeating tasks” is awkward; “repetitive tasks” is clearer. No punctuation-based rejection.
