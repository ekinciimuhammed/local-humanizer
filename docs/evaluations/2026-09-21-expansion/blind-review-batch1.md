# Blind meaning review: batch 1

Reviewed only `blind-batch1.json`, comparing each candidate directly with its source. A pass means the important meaning survives; it does not mean every wording choice is exact. Grammar is assessed separately, and punctuation alone does not cause rejection.

| Candidate | Important meaning | Main reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | PASS | Preserves the substantive claims, qualifications, causal relationship, and risk categories. |
| B | FAIL | Replaces conditional potential with an unsupported claim of demonstrated success and damages the employee-benefit relationship. |
| C | PASS | Preserves the substantive content; slightly strengthens the recommendation and weakens the description of AI's current importance. |
| D | PASS | Closely preserves the source; slightly strengthens the language about reducing harm. |
| E | FAIL | Broadens who receives the stated work benefit and turns a possible route to responsible value creation into a necessary condition. Several explicit claims also disappear. |

## A — PASS

- **Current role and scope:** “woven into working life and life outside work” retains AI's established role in professional and everyday life. “Changing how people and organizations operate” preserves both affected groups.
- **Functions and causality:** Repetitive work and assistance with complicated decisions remain. “Repetitive jobs are an obvious place to use it” makes automation less explicit, but the surrounding account of faster intelligent systems retains the relevant mechanism. This is a minor loss of precision.
- **Efficiency and beneficiaries:** Faster completion of formerly time-consuming, hands-on work leads to employees having more time for creative, strategic, and higher-value work. All important parts of this causal claim survive.
- **Risks and debate:** The questions preserve privacy, bias, job displacement, and automated-decision reliability as concerns under discussion. They do not assert that every possible harm will occur.
- **Adoption recommendation:** Organizations still need to consider possible gains and possible harms carefully.
- **Conditional value and mitigation:** “Used responsibly, AI could be worth a great deal” retains potential rather than guaranteed value. Ethics, technological advances, and human oversight remain the combination that can limit harm.
- **Unsupported detail:** None material. “Obvious” adds rhetorical emphasis, not a consequential factual claim.
- **Grammar:** Clear and grammatical. The rhetorical questions and informal wording are stylistic choices.

## B — FAIL

- **Critical unsupported outcome and lost conditionality:** “Organizations that use AI technology, ethical guidelines, and human judgment were successful” asserts a past success result that the source never supplies. The source offers potential significant value under responsible implementation, together with a way to reduce possible negative effects. Neither past success nor success for the described class of organizations is established.
- **Critical causal/agency distortion:** “AI can be used to focus on strategic and creative jobs” does not clearly preserve the source's claim that faster task completion frees **employees** to focus on those activities. The intervening “still use our work hard” is unintelligible, so it cannot reliably preserve the manual-effort claim. High-value work is omitted.
- **Functions and scope:** “Automate and make decisions” drops repetitive-task specificity and changes assistance with complex decision-making into making decisions. This is a material shift in AI's role. General involvement in life survives, but the current integral role and transformation of how individuals and organizations operate are weakened or absent.
- **Efficiency:** Improved efficiency and quicker work survive in broad form. The comparison with previously substantial time and manual effort does not survive clearly.
- **Risks and debate:** Privacy, bias, displacement, and correctness/reliability of decisions survive. Splitting “algorithmic bias” into “algorithms, bias” makes algorithms themselves an additional unspecified problem and weakens the precise bias category. Increasing adoption and continuing debate are omitted; those omissions are secondary to the failures above.
- **Adoption recommendation:** Considering benefits and proper use is retained, but the explicit instruction to consider both opportunities and potential risks is weakened. Earlier reference to disadvantages only partially compensates.
- **Responsible implementation:** Ethical guidelines and human judgment are related to, but less exact than, ethical considerations and human oversight. Technological innovation and reduction of potential negative effects are absent.
- **Grammar:** Multiple substantial problems: “provide benefits and disadvantages to use,” “still use our work hard,” and the tense mismatch in “Organizations that use ... were successful.” The meaning rejection is independently justified by the unsupported success claim; it is not a punctuation judgment.

## C — PASS

- **Current role and scope:** Professional and everyday contexts remain, as does transformation of operations. “Operates within” is weaker than “integral,” and individuals and organizations are not both explicitly named in the opening claim. These are minor compression losses rather than a different account of AI's reach.
- **Functions, efficiency, and causality:** Repetitive-task automation, support for complex decisions, reduced completion time relative to earlier manual work, and the resulting opportunity for employees to pursue creative, strategic, high-value work all survive.
- **Risks and debate:** All four named risk categories, expanding adoption, and continuing debate survive.
- **Adoption recommendation:** “Must carefully weigh” is stronger than “should carefully consider.” This adds normative force without claiming a new legal obligation or changing the recommended conduct; it is a minor fidelity issue.
- **Conditional value and mitigation:** Responsible implementation retains the potential for significant value, and innovation, ethics, oversight, and reduced negative effects all remain. There is no guarantee of success or claim of observed results.
- **Grammar:** Clear and grammatical. No material problem.

## D — PASS

- **Current role and scope:** “Core part” preserves “integral,” and professional/everyday life plus people/organizations remain explicit. “How ... work” is a little narrower than “how ... operate,” but the opening scope keeps the overall meaning intact.
- **Functions, efficiency, and causality:** Handling repetitive tasks, supporting complex decisions, faster completion of previously time- and effort-intensive tasks, and employees' resulting ability to focus on creative, strategic, high-value work all survive.
- **Risks and debate:** All four categories remain, alongside growing adoption and ongoing debate. “Serious” mildly intensifies “important.”
- **Adoption recommendation:** Weighing opportunities and potential risks remains. “Need to” is a modest strengthening of “should.”
- **Conditional value and mitigation:** “Can create significant value if ... implemented responsibly” retains conditional capability rather than asserting actual success. Innovation, ethics, and human oversight remain.
- **Minor overstatement:** “Minimizing possible negative effects” is stronger than “reducing potential negative effects”: reduction need not achieve the minimum attainable harm. In this general-language context, this is a minor fidelity issue, not enough to reject the important meaning. “Reducing” would be more exact.
- **Grammar:** Clear and grammatical. No material problem.

## E — FAIL

- **Material scope expansion:** The source says faster task completion allows **employees** to focus on creative, strategic, and high-value activities. The candidate says automation and decision support “frees individuals and organizations” to do so “in both professional and everyday life.” This extends the specific work-allocation benefit to organizations and everyday activity. The source's general claim about AI's presence in everyday life does not establish that broader causal benefit.
- **Material change from possibility to necessity:** “Realizing this potential value while mitigating harm requires responsible use that combines ... sustained human oversight” states that this particular combination is necessary. The source says organizations **can** use that combination to create value responsibly and reduce potential negative effects; it does not establish that it is a necessary or exclusive route. “Sustained” also introduces a continuity requirement not specified in “human oversight.”
- **Current role and transformation:** Professional and everyday life are mentioned, but their mention is attached to the broadened benefit above. The explicit claims that AI is already integral to both settings and is transforming how individuals and organizations operate are omitted. These omissions weaken coverage; they are not treated as standalone proof of critical distortion.
- **Functions and efficiency:** Automation of repetitive tasks and support for complex decisions survive. Reduced time and manual effort broadly preserve improved efficiency, although the historical comparison and faster completion claim become less explicit.
- **Risks and debate:** All four named concerns and ongoing debate remain. Their relationship to growing AI adoption is omitted. “Caution is essential” strengthens the source's advisory tone.
- **Adoption recommendation:** The explicit recommendation that organizations carefully consider both opportunities and risks when adopting AI is absent. Discussion of value and caution partly conveys the balance, so this is an omission rather than an outright reversal.
- **Conditional value and mitigation:** Potential value and mitigation of harm remain, as do innovation, ethics, and oversight. The potential for **significant** value is reduced to generic potential value. The necessity claim described above is the consequential change.
- **Grammar:** Grammatical overall. “Continue to generate ongoing debate” is redundant. The long second sentence compresses multiple relationships and enables the scope ambiguity; punctuation alone is not the reason for rejection.
