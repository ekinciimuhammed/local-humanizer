## T — PASS

**Coverage:** Work and everyday life, changing how people and groups function, repetitive-task handling, and assistance with difficult decisions remain. Improved efficiency, formerly substantial time and manual effort, and employees' resulting opportunity for creative, strategic, higher-value activities survive in plain language. Growing use, privacy, algorithmic unfairness, job displacement, decision reliability, ongoing debate, and weighing advantages against possible risks all remain. Significant possible benefits are still conditional on fair/careful implementation. New technology, ethics, human oversight, and reduction of possible adverse effects survive without an asserted successful outcome.

**Minor issues and concrete repairs:**

- “A part” weakens “integral”; “central” is closer. “Groups” is less exact than “organizations,” but does not erase organizational scope here.
- “Systems that can think” adds anthropomorphic capability wording absent from “intelligent systems.” In this broad nontechnical passage it can be read as a loose gloss on intelligent task processing, not an explicit claim of consciousness or human cognition, so it does not independently fail important meaning. Replace with “intelligent systems” to avoid the unsupported implication.
- “Work gets done faster” and “now handle tasks” are more affirmative about realized performance than “can now be completed faster.” Restore “can complete tasks faster” for exact capability framing. The draft does not claim all tasks are faster or supply invented measured results.
- “Dull” is an unsupported subjective addition. “Long-term planning” narrows strategic activity, which need not be long-term; use “strategic work.” “More important” is an imperfect but adequate general paraphrase of higher-value work.
- “The wins” is more definite than opportunities, though “both ... possible dangers” retains the overall benefits/risks balance. Use “possible benefits.” Advice about “using” AI loses the explicit adoption-decision context.
- “Fair and careful” reasonably expresses responsible use here. “Keep people ... retaining control” adds control to oversight; use “human oversight.” “New tech” broadly preserves innovation.

No names, numbers, results, or external attribution are invented. The important responsible-use qualification and possible-harm framing survive despite the loose wording.

## U — FAIL

**Material proposal-status loss:** The source explicitly says the library **is considering** the pilot, repeatedly uses “would,” and distinguishes **approval of the pilot** from a lasting extension. The draft presents “a six-week trial starting October 5,” states that services **will** be provided and staff **will** collect data, and ends with **starting** the trial rather than its approval. “The plan is” identifies a plan but does not retain that the pilot itself is still under consideration. Taken together, the wording presents a scheduled implementation rather than the source's unapproved proposal. Minimal repair: introduce “The library is considering a six-week pilot beginning October 5”; use “would” or “if approved” for planned operations; finish with “Approval of the pilot would not guarantee a permanent extension.” The existing allocation of funds remains a fact and need not be made conditional.

**Unsupported attribution:** “The library notes” attributes the representativeness caveat to an explicit library acknowledgment. The source gives the caveat without that attribution. Remove “The library notes that.” Unlike a definite-exclusion claim, the draft correctly retains **might not reflect**; its uncertainty should remain.

**Coverage otherwise:** Northbridge Library; $4,800 for additional staffing; six weeks; October 5; Tuesdays and Thursdays; 9 pm rather than 6 pm; improved access for those unable to attend during normal work hours; no Sunday opening; no decision about continuation; continued daytime services; counts during additional hours; optional feedback; demand assessment; possible failure to reflect nonattending residents; end-of-trial committee review of attendance, staffing cost, and feedback; deciding whether to recommend permanence; and no guarantee of a permanent extension all survive. There are no numerical errors. The draft does not improperly give the committee final approval power.

**Minor imprecision:** Continued daytime services lack the explicit “throughout the six weeks” boundary, although the trial context supplies it. “People actually want” adds emphasis but reasonably expresses demand. “Residents who do not come in” is an acceptable loose description of nonattendees here.

## V — PASS

**Coverage:** Bellfield Design and its recent employee hybrid-work survey remain. The 84 invited employees, 56 responses, and 39 within that responding group reporting better concentration at home are accurate. The result remains limited to some people and does not establish whole-company productivity gains. Communication difficulty across locations and usefulness of scheduled online meetings remain attributed to staff. The survey's omission of a home/office work-quality comparison remains. Three more months of the current schedule, gathering more feedback, voluntary future participation, self-report and nonresponse limitations, cautious interpretation, and the need for additional evidence on overall performance all survive.

**Minor issues and concrete repairs:**

- “These results show ... might help” is slightly stronger than “suggest ... may,” but the explicit “might” and immediate productivity disclaimer preserve the tentative inference. “Suggest” is more exact.
- “Managers will keep” strengthens a plan into a firmer future statement. Use “Managers plan to keep.” In this isolated statement of a future management plan, with no approval process or competing decision status in the source, this is minor rather than a critical change.
- “Some people” weakens the specificity of “several respondents”; “several respondents” is exact. The surrounding survey context still ties these reports to participants.
- “Kept them up to date” is slightly stronger than “helped them stay informed”; retain “helped.” “Tougher to have casual chats” is slightly broader than harder to arrange them.
- “Internal” is not explicit, but a company asking its own staff keeps that scope clear. “More proof” is stronger than “further evidence”; use “more evidence.” No proof of an effect is claimed.

No critical omission, denominator error, new outcome, or invented attribution appears.
