# Blind meaning review: batch 2

Reviewed only `blind-batch2.json` for this batch. Plain-language equivalents are acceptable. Meaning and grammar are assessed separately; punctuation alone is not grounds for failure.

| Candidate | Important meaning | Main failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| F | FAIL | Eliminates manual work rather than describing faster completion; loses current professional/everyday scope and changes conditional potential value to a comparative performance claim. |
| G | FAIL | Adds necessary conditions and an assurance of universal fairness, safety, and absence of hidden harm; omits the explicit adoption tradeoff. |
| H | FAIL | Changes growing use to overuse, invents majority prevalence, and drops human oversight from responsible implementation. |

## F — FAIL

### Meaning issues and minimal repair

1. **“Removes the need for hands-on work” overstates the benefit.** The source says work previously requiring substantial time and manual effort can be completed faster, not that manual work becomes unnecessary. Replace this with “can finish tasks that once took substantial time and hands-on effort more quickly.”
2. **The opening loses the present scope and transformation claims.** There is no equivalent of AI being integral to professional **and everyday life**, or of its transforming how individuals and organizations operate. Add one short sentence retaining these claims.
3. **“AI works best when companies use it carefully” introduces an unsupported comparative conclusion.** The source promises neither best performance nor observed success; it describes potential significant value under responsible implementation. Replace with “Used responsibly, AI could create substantial value.”
4. **The employee benefit is incomplete.** “Creative and important thinking” preserves creative and broadly high-value work, but loses strategic work and narrows activities to thinking. Use “creative, strategic, and higher-value work.”
5. **“Answers the machine gives” broadens the reliability concern.** The source specifically concerns automated **decisions**. Replace “answers” with “decisions.”

The draft retains repetitive-task handling, decision support, the efficiency-to-worker-benefit causal link, privacy, unfair treatment, possible job loss, continuing debate, and consideration of risks and benefits. “Companies” is narrower than “organizations”; use the latter where general advice is intended. “Must ... before” slightly strengthens “should ... associated with adopting”; “should ... when considering adoption” is closer. Increasing use as the context for concerns is omitted; add “As AI use grows” to the risk paragraph.

The final paragraph reasonably conveys innovation, ethics, oversight, and possible mitigation. “Retain control” goes beyond oversight but does not independently cause the failure. “Lowering the chance” describes reduced probability rather than the broader reduction of negative effects; “reducing possible harm” is more exact. “Boring” is an unsupported subjective descriptor and can simply be deleted.

### Grammar

Generally grammatical. “Boring, repeating tasks” is awkward; “repetitive tasks” is clearer. No punctuation-based rejection.

## G — FAIL

### Meaning issues and minimal repair

1. **The final sentence adds a materially stronger outcome:** “ensuring ... it serves everyone fairly and safely without hidden harm.” The source offers a way to **reduce possible negative effects**, not to ensure universal fairness, safety, or absence of harm. Delete this sentence, or replace its outcome with “helping reduce possible negative effects.” “Not just adding new gadgets” also introduces a contrast absent from the source.
2. **“Organizations must mix ... To use AI well” changes an available approach into a requirement.** Replace the opening of that sentence with “By combining technological innovation with ethical considerations and human oversight, organizations can ...”. The plain-language versions of these concepts are acceptable, but the original does not establish this combination as necessary for every successful use.
3. **The adoption recommendation is missing.** Add: “Organizations should carefully weigh the opportunities and potential risks when considering AI adoption.” General advice about using AI well does not express this decision-stage tradeoff.
4. **Professional/everyday scope is missing, and “workers and companies” narrows “individuals and organizations.”** Add the current integral role in professional and everyday life, and use “people and organizations” for those whose operations are changing.
5. **Conditional significant value is weakened to “gain real benefits.”** State that responsible use **could create substantial value**, while reducing possible harm. This preserves both the degree of value and its potential status.

The central efficiency claim and its causal connection to staff doing other work survive. “Creative and important plans” broadly conveys creativity and value, but strategic work is only implicit and work is narrowed to plans; “creative, strategic, and higher-value work” repairs this efficiently. “Long hours and hard labor” adds physical/intensity connotations absent from “significant time and manual effort”; restore the latter meaning with “a lot of time and hands-on effort.”

Growing adoption, ongoing debate, and all four risk themes survive. “Four main risks” adds ranking/exhaustiveness to the source's illustrative “such as”; use “risks such as.” “Decisions made without people” may imply an entirely human-free decision process, whereas automated decisions can involve oversight; “automated decisions” is more exact. “Helps avoid bad results” alone could reasonably mean mitigation, but the final assurance of serving everyone safely without hidden harm makes the overall outcome substantially stronger than the source.

### Grammar

Generally grammatical. “Tasks ... can now finish” is less natural than “tasks ... can now be finished.” No punctuation-based rejection.

## H — FAIL

### Meaning issues and minimal repair

1. **“The majority of people” invents a quantitative prevalence claim.** Replace the first sentence with “AI is now an integral part of professional and everyday life.”
2. **“AI can change the way people do things” weakens an ongoing transformation to mere capability and omits organizations.** Use “AI is changing how people and organizations operate” while retaining the automation and decision-support explanation.
3. **“Overuse of AI ... raises some concerns” changes the trigger.** The source links concerns to **growing use**, not only excessive use. Replace “Overuse” with “Growing use.”
4. **The last listed concern is the activity rather than its reliability:** “automated decision making.” Replace with “the reliability of automated decisions”; qualify “bias” as “algorithmic bias.” Retain that these concerns continue to generate debate, which is currently missing.
5. **Human oversight disappears from the responsible-implementation account.** “Being responsible for the technology” is not an equivalent of people overseeing AI. Explicitly retain innovation, ethics, and human oversight as the combination that can help organizations use AI and reduce possible harm.
6. **“Avoid the negative consequences” loses the source's degree and uncertainty.** Use “reduce potential negative effects.” Although “can help ... avoid” is qualified, it still targets avoidance rather than reduction and refers to negative consequences as definite.

The efficiency-to-more-creative/strategic-work causal relationship broadly survives, but the beneficiary changes from **employees** to people generally, and high-value activities disappear. Use “freeing employees to focus on creative, strategic, and high-value work.” “By AI” also slightly strengthens “with intelligent systems”; “with AI” preserves assistance as well as automation.

The benefits-versus-risks advice and conditional claim that responsible AI use can be very valuable survive. “This is an issue for companies that use AI in any way” adds an unsupported universal assertion about every form of company use; delete it. “Companies” narrows the general adoption recommendation; use “organizations” and retain careful consideration when adopting AI.

### Grammar

“It makes tasks done faster” is ungrammatical; use “It allows tasks to be completed faster.” “Making people free to do” is awkward; use “freeing employees to focus on.” Other sentences are readable. The meaning failure is independent of these grammar issues.
