## W — FAIL

### Material changes

1. **Objective strengthened:** “The goal is to stop members from buying tools they rarely use” is stronger than reducing their **need** to buy such tools. The source does not aim to eliminate purchases or discourage buying regardless of need. Minimal repair: “The goal is to reduce members' need to buy tools they rarely use.”
2. **Evidential limit weakened:** “If few people borrow tools, it might not mean there is no demand” leaves the relation open. The source explicitly says low borrowing figures **alone would not establish** absence of need. Retaining a possible alternative explanation does not fully retain that restriction on inference. Minimal repair: “Low borrowing figures alone would not show that members had no need for shared tools, because limited collection times might prevent some from participating.”

### Other concrete fidelity issues

- “Members could borrow 12 hand tools” preserves the number but is ambiguous between a collection of 12 and an individual borrowing allowance. “A collection of 12 hand tools would be available to members” preserves the inventory scope exactly. The draft does not unambiguously assert a per-member allowance, so this is not independently treated as a numerical error.
- “They can only do so if the tool ... has not been taken already” makes the availability condition attach to attempting unfamiliar gardening jobs, rather than specifically accessing the shared equipment. Members could try jobs using other tools; the source restricts scheme access. Use “Access to a shared tool would depend on whether it had already been borrowed.”
- “Must report if something breaks” strengthens an expectation into a requirement and narrows damage to breakage. Use “Members would be expected to report damage.”
- “Still debating who pays” adds an active debate not established by “has not yet decided how repair costs would be allocated.” Use “has not yet decided how repair costs would be shared.”
- “Mark it when it comes back” could mean marking the tool physically rather than recording its return. Use “record its return.”
- “The committee will” is firmer than “would,” but the explicit “might try” opening preserves the proposal status overall. “Would” would be more consistent.

### Coverage that survives

Westmere Garden Club; a proposed rather than already approved scheme; April 10; eight weeks; Saturday mornings; the number 12; hand tools and exclusion of powered equipment; unchanged membership costs; possible assistance to newer gardeners; no guarantee of requested availability; volunteer checks before lending; unresolved repair-cost allocation; written arrangements required before starting; end-of-trial review of borrowing, upkeep costs, and member comments; a decision on continuation informed by that review; and limited pickup times potentially preventing participation all remain. There are no invented dates, prices, or measured outcomes. The explicit evidential qualification identified above is the main lost limitation.

### Grammar, separate from meaning

Generally readable and grammatical. “Mark it” is chiefly a meaning ambiguity. Punctuation did not affect the verdict.

## X — PASS

### Coverage

- **Identity and study:** The fictional Eastborough Transit Group and its pilot survey of experiences with a revised bus timetable remain. “Trial survey” is an acceptable plain-language equivalent of pilot survey.
- **Counts and attribution:** 120 invited, 72 respondents, and 46 **within that respondent group** reporting more convenient usual journeys are preserved. No denominator is changed, and reported convenience does not become measured operational improvement.
- **Reliability limitation:** The draft explicitly states that the answers do not prove more reliable bus arrivals. “Normal trips easier” reasonably conveys greater convenience in this context.
- **Mixed findings:** Earlier morning departure receives favorable reports, while longer evening service intervals make connections harder. Both remain participant reports, rather than universal outcomes.
- **Missing journeys and scope:** The absence of questions about trips no longer taken remains, along with the resulting inability to determine effects on people who switched transport modes.
- **Next steps and decision status:** Four further weeks with the timetable unchanged, separate arrival-time records, optional participation in another questionnaire, and no decision about permanent retention all survive.
- **Evidence limits:** Potential usefulness for further investigation, lack of representativeness for every passenger, potentially different nonrespondent experiences, and the need for operational records alongside passenger feedback remain explicit.

### Minor wording differences

- “Will leave” is firmer than “plans to keep”; use “plans to leave.” The separately stated lack of a permanent decision preserves the important decision boundary.
- “Some people” is less precise than “several respondents,” though the survey context preserves respondent attribution. “Several respondents” is exact.
- “If another questionnaire is sent out” makes the conditional future questionnaire explicit; this is compatible with the source's “would remain optional.”
- “Kept forever” is a more colloquial and slightly more absolute rendering of permanent retention; “retained permanently” is closer.
- “Running records” is less clear than “operational records,” but in the context of separate bus-arrival logs the intended operational evidence is recoverable.

No important omission, unsupported result, changed numerical detail, or material strengthening of an evidential claim appears.

### Grammar, separate from meaning

Clear and broadly grammatical. “Running records” is awkward terminology. Punctuation did not affect the verdict.
