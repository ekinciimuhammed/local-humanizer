# Blind meaning review: guided candidate

**Important meaning: PASS.** Reviewed only `blind-guided.json` for this review. The important scope, agency, causal relationships, risks, and conditional value claim survive. Remaining differences are minor fidelity issues, not critical distortions.

## Meaning preserved

- AI's central current role in both work and everyday life, and its changing how people and organizations operate, remain explicit.
- Handling repetitive tasks and helping with difficult decisions preserve automation and decision support in plain language.
- Faster task completion frees workers for creative, strategic, and high-value activities. Both employee agency and the causal connection survive.
- Growing adoption and continuing debate remain. The questions preserve privacy, algorithmic unfairness, job displacement, and automated-decision reliability as concerns; they do not turn affirmative evidence of realized harms into uncertainty.
- Organizations are still asked to consider benefits and risks.
- “Could create a lot of value ... responsibly” preserves substantial potential value under responsible implementation, without inventing observed success or guaranteeing outcomes.
- New technology, ethical considerations, and human oversight remain in plain language, along with reduction of potential negative effects.

## Remaining omissions and minor fidelity issues

1. **Historical manual-effort comparison omitted:** “Saves time and makes tasks faster” retains efficiency but does not explicitly say that these tasks formerly required considerable time and manual effort. For exact coverage: “Tasks that once required substantial time and manual effort can now be completed faster with AI.” This omission does not remove the main efficiency claim or its worker benefit.
2. **Recommendation strengthened:** “Must ... before using” is firmer and more temporally restrictive than “should carefully consider ... associated with adopting.” “Organizations should carefully weigh the opportunities and potential risks when considering AI adoption” is closer.
3. **Possible risks described as dangers:** “These dangers” is a little more definite than “potential risks,” although the preceding questions preserve uncertainty. Use “these potential risks.”
4. **Innovation simplified:** “New tools” is narrower than technological innovation, but conveys the general idea adequately in this context. “Technological innovation” is exact.
5. **Control added to oversight:** “Keep watch and retain control” includes oversight while adding ongoing human control. The source only specifies human oversight. “People provide oversight” avoids the addition.
6. **Responsible use framed as a definition:** “This means mixing ...” sounds more definitional than the source's description of an available approach. It does not explicitly assert exclusivity or necessity, so it is a minor shift. “Combining ... can help organizations ...” would preserve the source's relation more precisely.

## Grammar and wording

Readable and broadly grammatical. “Central now in work,” “makes tasks faster,” and “handling repeating tasks” are awkward. Prefer “now central to work,” “helps complete tasks faster,” and “handling repetitive tasks.” No punctuation issue warrants rejection.
