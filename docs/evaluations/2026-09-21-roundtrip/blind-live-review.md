## Minor fidelity issues

1. **“Systems that think”** adds anthropomorphic wording absent from “intelligent systems.” In this broad nontechnical context it can mean intelligent task processing; it does not expressly claim consciousness or human cognition. Replace with “intelligent systems” to avoid the unsupported implication.
2. **“Dull”** is an unsupported subjective characterization of repetitive tasks. Delete it.
3. **“Long-term planning”** narrows strategic work, which can also concern nearer-term decisions. Use “strategic work.” “More important” is a less exact substitute for higher-value work, but the general benefit remains.
4. **“Groups”** is less precise than organizations, and “jobs and daily routines” slightly narrows professional and everyday life. Neither removes the important scope here.
5. **“Work gets done faster”** is more affirmative than “can now be completed faster,” although the following sentence explicitly retains “can.” The overall passage still conveys capability without inventing measured results.
6. **“Good chances”** is an awkward substitute for opportunities. The recommendation also omits explicit careful consideration and the adoption-decision context. “Organizations should carefully weigh the opportunities and potential risks when considering adoption” is exact.
7. **“Retaining control”** strengthens human oversight. “Human oversight” avoids adding a requirement for continued control. “Moral approach” broadly preserves ethics, while “new tech” loosely conveys technological innovation.

No critical omission, fabricated outcome, altered beneficiary, or loss of the responsible-use qualification. There are no names, quantities, or external attributions to misstate.
